Welcome to Kasaragod

Where History
Meets the Sea

Kasaragod is a land of extraordinary diversity — the only district in India where seven languages coexist harmoniously. From the imposing Bekal Fort overlooking the Arabian Sea to the tranquil Valiyaparamba backwaters, every corner tells a story steeped in centuries of culture, trade and coastal heritage.

Must-Visit Places

Places to Discover
in Kasaragod

From ancient forts overlooking the sea to sacred lake temples — Kasaragod's gems are unlike anywhere else in Kerala.

01 — Fort

Bekal Fort

Kerala's largest fort, built in the 17th century on a promontory jutting into the Arabian Sea. The observation tower offers breathtaking 360° views of the coastline, backwaters and horizon.

History · Coastal

02 — Beach

Kappil Beach

A rare and magical confluence where the Kappil backwater lake meets the Arabian Sea. The wooden bridge stretching over the water is one of Kerala's most photographed landmarks.

Beach · Backwaters

03 — Temple

Ananthapura Lake Temple

The only lake temple in Kerala, believed to be the original seat of Ananthapadmanabha. The temple sits on a small island surrounded by a sacred lake, home to a guardian crocodile that has lived there for centuries.

Spiritual · Unique

04 — Backwaters

Valiyaparamba Backwaters

One of Kerala's four major backwater systems — a serene network of islands, canals and coconut groves far less crowded than Alleppey. Houseboat stays here feel truly off the beaten path.

Backwaters · Peaceful

05 — Fort

Chandragiri Fort

A 17th century fort perched above the confluence of the Chandragiri River and the Arabian Sea. The views from the ramparts at sunset are among the most dramatic in all of Kerala.

History · Sunset

06 — Hill Station

Ranipuram

Often called the Ooty of Kasaragod, Ranipuram rises to 750 metres with grassy meadows, shola forests and mist-covered trekking trails rarely visited by tourists — a true hidden highland.

Trekking · Nature

07 — Heritage

Malik Deenar Mosque

One of India's oldest mosques, believed to have been built by Malik Ibn Dinar, a companion of Prophet Muhammad, in the 7th century. An extraordinary piece of living history on the Malabar coast.

Heritage · Spiritual

08 — Beach

Bekal Beach

A long, unspoiled beach stretching beside the fort, framed by casuarina trees and gentle waves. Far quieter than Kerala's famous southern beaches, perfect for long early morning walks.

Beach · Relaxed

Flavour Trail · Kasaragod

Taste of the North

Kasaragod sits at the crossroads of Kerala, Karnataka and Tulu cultures — a culinary meeting point where coconut, spice and tradition create dishes you won't find anywhere else.

Breakfast · Kasaragod

Pathiri

Thin, soft rice flour flatbreads — a Malabar Muslim staple. Eaten with spiced chicken or mutton curry, the texture is delicate and unlike anything else in Kerala.

Malabar · Traditional

Coastal · Kasaragod

Fish Gassi

A Tulu-influenced coconut fish curry with a deep, roasted spice base. Richer and smokier than Kerala's typical fish curry — a legacy of Kasaragod's cross-cultural cooking.

Seafood · Tulu

Breakfast · Tulu Influence

Neer Dosa

Paper-thin, lacy white rice crepes — softer and more delicate than regular dosa, with a melt-in-the-mouth texture. Served with coconut chutney and egg curry in most Kasaragod homes.

Vegetarian · Light

Sweets · Kasaragod

Kasaragod Halwa

Similar to Kozhikode halwa but with its own local character — intensely sweet, dense and jewel-coloured, made with wheat starch, coconut oil and generous ghee. A gift worth carrying home.

Dessert · Specialty

What To Do

Experiences in Kasaragod

Beyond sightseeing — Kasaragod offers experiences that connect you with the land, the sea and the people who call this remarkable frontier home.

Fort Trail Walk

Walk the full perimeter of Bekal Fort at sunrise. The moat, the observation tower and the views of the sea at golden hour are unforgettable.

Backwater Kayaking

Paddle through the Valiyaparamba islands at dawn — through mangroves, past fishing villages and under canopies of coconut palms in near-total silence.

Yakshagana Performance

Watch a live Yakshagana — the elaborate classical dance-theatre of the Tulu people. Elaborate costumes, percussion and mythology merge in a spectacle unique to this region.

Ranipuram Trek

A moderate 6km trek through dense forest to the summit at 750m. The grasslands at the top feel like a different world — cool, misty and utterly isolated.

Malabar Cooking Class

Learn to make pathiri, fish gassi and coconut chutney in a local home. Kasaragod's cross-cultural cuisine is best understood with your hands in the spice jar.

Cashew Farm Visit

Visit a traditional cashew estate during harvest season (Feb–May) and see the full process from fruit to nut, with tasting of fresh cashew feni and jaggery.

Best season
Good season

Oct – Feb — Peak season. Cool and dry, perfect for beaches, fort walks and trekking. Book accommodation early during Christmas and New Year.

Feb – Apr — Cashew harvest season. The best time to visit the estates and experience the region's most iconic agricultural tradition.

Jun – Sep — Monsoon season. The fort and backwaters take on a dramatic, moody character. Fewer tourists, lower prices, lush greenery.

Theyyam season runs Nov – May in nearby Kannur. Combine both destinations for the full north Kerala experience.
